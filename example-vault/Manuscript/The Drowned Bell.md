The bell rang at the turn of the tide, and Maren was the only one on the wall who looked up.

She had been measuring the harbour mouth since dawn — chain, knot, chain, knot — and her fingers were raw from the wet line. Below her the fishing boats rocked in their moorings like sleeping dogs. The gulls had gone quiet. She noticed that first, before the sound: the gulls had gone quiet, and the water in the outer basin had turned the colour of pewter.

Then it rang again. Low, slow, a sound she felt in her teeth more than heard. It came from beneath the water, from where the old temple had stood before the sea took it a hundred years ago.

"Did you hear that?" she asked the dock-warden.

He looked at her the way people in Kesthaven always looked at cartographers: as if she had asked him to describe the colour of a debt. "Hear what?"

She marked the time in the margin of her chart. Fourth hour past noon, Highsun, the year forty-two. Later, when the city was ash and the harbour full of black hulls, she would look at that note and wonder why she had written it down so neatly.
